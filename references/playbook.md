# Xiaohongshu Copywriting Playbook

This reference is written in English for encoding stability. Generated Xiaohongshu posts should usually be in natural Chinese unless the user asks otherwise.

## Post Types

### Experience Note

Use when the user has a personal story, product trial, trip, course, tool, service, or lifestyle change.

Structure:

1. Scene: who I am or what problem I had.
2. Turning point: what I tried and why.
3. Details: process, cost, timeline, mistakes, comparison.
4. Result: specific outcome with realistic caveats.
5. Takeaway: who it suits and who should skip it.

### Tutorial Or Checklist

Use when the post should earn saves.

Structure:

1. Outcome promise.
2. Step list or decision tree.
3. Common mistakes.
4. Example or template.
5. Save cue.

### Review Or Comparison

Use when the user wants to evaluate products or tools.

Structure:

1. Test context.
2. Criteria.
3. Strengths and weaknesses.
4. Best-fit audience.
5. Buying or usage advice.

### Launch Or Commercial Note

Use when the user promotes a product, service, class, or offer.

Structure:

1. Pain point scene.
2. Why existing solutions are annoying.
3. What this offer changes.
4. Proof or details.
5. Soft CTA.

Keep the post useful even if the reader does not buy.

## Title Patterns

Use 5-10 variants, mixing angles:

- Pain point: "Why do your notes get views but no saves?"
- Result: "I used this checklist to plan one week of posts in 3 hours."
- Mistake: "The mistake I would avoid before learning viral titles."
- Checklist: "7 things I check before posting."
- Contrast: "Why this seeding post feels more trustworthy."
- Audience: "A low-cost content review method for small teams."
- Time or budget: "One hour to turn scattered notes into a usable RED post."
- Anti-hype: "No mysticism: how to make a post worth saving."

When writing Chinese titles, prefer under 28 Chinese characters when possible. Make the first 10-14 characters carry the hook.

## Opening Hooks

Strong hooks do one of these:

- Name a specific reader.
- Show a credible result.
- Start with a mistake.
- Create useful tension.
- Give a checklist promise.

Avoid generic openings equivalent to:

- "Today I want to share..."
- "Everyone must read this..."
- "This is really so good..."

## Body Patterns

Use short sections with Chinese labels. Useful labels include:

- Bottom line first.
- Who this suits.
- How I judge it.
- Mistakes I made.
- A template you can copy.
- Do not do this.
- Final checklist.

Keep each paragraph to 1-3 short lines. Use bullets when the user needs scanning or saving.

## Hashtag Mix

Use 8-15 tags:

- Category tags: platform operation, content marketing, skincare review, travel guide, etc.
- Audience tags: beginner creator, working women, small team startup, students, parents, etc.
- Scenario tags: pre-posting checklist, commute outfit, trip planning, home office, etc.
- Intent tags: useful tips, mistake avoidance, product review, checklist, template, etc.
- Brand or product tags only when supplied by the user.

Do not attach unrelated trending tags.

## Quality Checklist

Before finalizing, check:

- The first screen says who it is for and why to keep reading.
- The body has at least one concrete detail: number, process, example, comparison, price, time, setting, or limitation.
- The claims are supported by user-provided facts or softened as opinion.
- The CTA matches the goal and does not sound pushy.
- The tone is consistent with the user's brand or requested voice.
- The post has a reason to be saved, commented on, or shared.

## Rewrite Diagnosis Rubric

When improving a draft, diagnose the top issues:

- Hook too vague.
- Too commercial too early.
- Lacks scene or reader identity.
- Claims are too strong for the evidence.
- Paragraphs are dense.
- Hashtags are broad or unrelated.
- CTA is absent, pushy, or mismatched.

Then rewrite with the minimum necessary explanation.
